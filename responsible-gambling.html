Warning signs

If these sound familiar, stop, set a limit or self-exclude, and get independent help.

Chasing losses

Trying to recover losses by gambling again or placing larger bets.

Exceeding limits

Spending more time or money than planned, or finding it hard to stop a session.

Borrowing to gamble

Using money needed for essentials, borrowing, or using credit to keep gambling.

Hiding behavior

Concealing gambling, money spent or losses from family and friends.

Neglecting responsibilities

Missing work, family commitments or other activities because of gambling.

Emotional dependence

Using gambling to escape stress or low mood, or feeling restless when unable to gamble.

Safer play

Set a money and time limit before you start, and stop when you hit either. Don't gamble when alcohol, drugs, stress or anger are affecting your judgement. Take breaks, and keep other things in your life that don't involve betting.

For friends and family

If you are concerned about someone’s gambling, choose a calm time to talk and focus on specific changes you have noticed.

  • Listen without blaming or arguing about every past loss.
  • Encourage independent help and account limits or self-exclusion.
  • Protect shared finances where possible, and avoid lending money to cover gambling debts.
  • Consider bank gambling blocks and device controls if they are available.
  • Seek support for yourself; family-focused services such as Gam-Anon may help.

Protecting minors

Online gambling is for adults aged 18 or over, or the legal age where you live. Parents and guardians can reduce underage access by:

  • Using parental controls to block gambling sites and apps on devices minors can use.
  • Not leaving a 1win account logged in on a shared device.
  • Explaining that you can lose money, including in games that look free to play.
  • Keeping payment methods and account recovery details out of reach.

Age checks: KYC may come up, but it doesn't replace parental controls. Don't create or fund a gambling account for a minor.
