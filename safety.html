Safety Overview

This page is the license, KYC and dispute notes — not a second scored review. Game variety, the 500% package, payout clocks and the app live on their own URLs. Overall 4.9 stays on the homepage review.

What we can pin down: the published license number, TLS on the pages we opened, optional 2FA, KYC at first withdrawal (26 hours in our cashier test), and the absence of a named ADR. We did not inspect 1win’s bank accounts or run a laboratory RNG audit.

1win has operated since 2016 under a Curacao license. That framework is lighter than UK or Malta. It requires operators to keep player funds separate from operating money, offer responsible-gambling tools and log disputes — those are license claims, not a 1winex bank audit.

KYC & Identity Verification

Know Your Customer (KYC) is requested at the first withdrawal, not at signup. That is the same path we walked on the cashier: ID plus proof of address. Payouts stay held until documents land.

In our test, KYC cleared in 26 hours. Operators often quote 24–48 hours; treat our clock as one cashier run, not a guarantee. Three-rail payout table: payments.

Delayed KYC lets you look around before documents. Submit them before a large cash-out if you want to avoid a freeze at withdrawal.

Licensing & Regulation

1win operates under Curacao license 8048/JAZ, listed for MFI Investments Limited. The homepage review names Antillephone N.V. as the issuing provider — the same Curacao eGaming channel, not a second license. Confirm the number on the live listing. We are not the registry.

Curacao is real and weaker than the UK Gambling Commission or Malta Gaming Authority. It does not give UK/US/AU player protections. Disputes go through the operator first, then the Curacao channel — not a UK-style ADR.

A notable limitation: Curacao does not mandate affiliation with an independent Alternative Dispute Resolution (ADR) body. This means unresolved disputes must be escalated to the operator or, failing that, to Curacao eGaming directly — a slower path than the ADR-mediated process available to UK or MGA-licensed players.

Security Measures

We opened the site over HTTPS. The certificate we saw used TLS 1.3. No mixed-content warnings on the registration, login and cashier pages we used. That is a transport check, not a penetration test.

Account-level security includes optional two-factor authentication (2FA). Turn it on. The platform can flag odd logins (new device, unusual location). We did not test every freeze rule.

Player-fund segregation is a Curacao license requirement. We did not inspect 1win’s bank accounts. Do not read “9.7 fund protection” into this page — we removed that score. No GDPR certification is advertised.

We did not find 1win in public breach dumps at review time. That is an absence of a listing, not a guarantee.

Fair Play & RNG

Fairness at 1win is split. Third-party slots and live games use the studios’ own RNGs (Pragmatic Play, Evolution, NetEnt and others). Those labs sit with the providers, not as a 1win site-wide certificate.

1win Originals (Lucky Jet and similar in-house titles) are provably fair. Aviator is Spribe, not an Original — same split as the game catalog. We checked that Originals expose a seed-verify flow; we did not run a laboratory RNG audit.

1win does not publish a site-wide RTP audit. If you need an aggregate fairness report, that gap still stands.

Responsible Gambling Safeguards

Responsible gambling tools are a key safety indicator. 1win provides a functional set of player-control features. While not as extensive as those mandated by UK or Swedish regulators, the available tools cover the core harm-prevention needs.
